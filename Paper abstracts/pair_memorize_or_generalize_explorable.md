## Abstract
**Summary (no formal abstract):** This interactive PAIR explorable examines "grokking" — the phenomenon in which neural networks abruptly transition from memorizing their training data to generalizing on unseen inputs after extended training past zero training loss. Using minimal models trained on modular arithmetic and simple binary-classification tasks, the authors apply mechanistic interpretability to reverse-engineer the algorithms the networks discover, revealing periodic patterns and circular embeddings that implement the underlying group operation. The explorable walks readers through how weight decay biases optimization toward sparse, generalizable circuits; visualizes the transition from dense memorizing solutions to clean Fourier-style features; and exposes the hyperparameter regimes that produce memorization, grokking, or immediate generalization. It closes by discussing whether mechanistic insights from these toy systems extrapolate to large contemporary models, framing the work as a contribution to interpretability and the science of emergent capabilities.
