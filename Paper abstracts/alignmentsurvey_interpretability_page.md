## Abstract
Summary (no formal abstract):

The page is a curated educational survey of interpretability research situated within the "Assurance" pillar of the AI Alignment Survey. It opens with the definition: "Interpretability is a research field that makes machine learning systems and their decision-making process understandable to human beings," followed by the claim that "Interpretability research builds a toolbox with which something novel about the models can be better described or predicted." The body of the page is organised into three major sections. (1) *Techniques to Analyze Neural Networks*, covering circuit analysis, attribution methods, visualisation, perturbation and ablation, and representation mapping. (2) *Making Interpretable Models*, addressing replacement of black-box components with interpretable ones and mechanistic investigation of trained networks. (3) *Challenges and Criticisms*, discussing superposition, scalability of interpretability methods, and the limitations of toy-model studies. Each section is accompanied by short annotated paper recommendations. The page functions as a study guide and entry point for researchers approaching interpretability from an alignment-assurance perspective rather than as a primary research contribution.
